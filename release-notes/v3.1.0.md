Alerts on your phone. Your watch list and settings carry over.

**Already on 3.0?** The app offers this update by itself within a day, or now from **Settings → Updates → Check for updates**: one click, and it restarts on 3.1.

**New here?** Download [`UnifiStockWatcher-Setup.exe`](https://github.com/jamesccupps/UnifiStockWatcher/releases/download/v3.1.0/UnifiStockWatcher-Setup.exe) (installs for your Windows user, no admin prompt) or the portable [`UnifiStockWatcher-win64.zip`](https://github.com/jamesccupps/UnifiStockWatcher/releases/download/v3.1.0/UnifiStockWatcher-win64.zip). The app isn't code-signed, so Windows may say *Windows protected your PC*: **More info → Run anyway**.

### New

- **Alerts on your phone.** **Settings → Alerts on your phone** sends every in-stock alert to **ntfy**, **Pushover**, **Discord** or **Slack**, written for that app (the product, its price and store, and a link that opens it), or as **JSON to any address**: Home Assistant, n8n, Zapier and the like. ntfy is free and needs no account.
- **Send a test** shows what the service answered, like *HTTP 404 (Unknown Webhook)* for a deleted Discord webhook. The Activity view notes every alert sent, or why one wasn't; one that can't get through is tried twice more.
- The **command-line watcher** sends them too, from the same settings, and `--test` sends a test.

How to set up each service, and the JSON's fields: [README, Alerts on your phone](https://github.com/jamesccupps/UnifiStockWatcher#alerts-on-your-phone).

Your PC sends the alerts, so it has to be on and watching. The addresses and keys stay in `settings.json` in your data folder, and the log only ever names the service or the host.
